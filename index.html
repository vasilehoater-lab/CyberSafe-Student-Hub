Think before you click

The internet is useful for studying, shopping and keeping in touch. A few careful habits can help protect your accounts and personal information.

STOP. CHECK. THEN CLICK.

Learn the basics

1. Spot phishing

Phishing messages try to trick you into sharing passwords, bank details or other private information.

  • Check the sender and full web address.
  • Be careful with urgent messages.
  • Do not open unexpected attachments or links.
  • When unsure, use the organisation's official website.

2. Use strong passwords

Use a long, unique password or passphrase for each important account.

  • Do not reuse your email password.
  • Never share your password.
  • Consider a trusted password manager.

3. Turn on MFA

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds another check when you sign in.

  • Enable MFA where available.
  • Never approve a sign-in you did not start.
  • Keep recovery details up to date.

About this website

CyberSafe Student Hub is an educational prototype sharing basic online-safety advice with students. It demonstrates a simple website, an interactive checklist and a short quiz.

Important: This website is for learning only. It does not scan links, detect real phishing attacks, store answers or collect personal information. Follow your school or organisation's process to report suspicious messages.
